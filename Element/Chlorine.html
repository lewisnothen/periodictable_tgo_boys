Chlorine

Chlorine

Chlorine is a chemical element with the symbol Cl and atomic number 17. It is a non-metal and belongs to the halogen group on the periodic table. Chlorine is a pale yellow-green gas at room temperature. It is highly reactive and is commonly found combined with other elements.

Chemical Properties

See the color, test the mass, solid, liquid, or a gas!

Data
  • Reactivity: Highly reactive, especially with metals and hydrogen
  • Group: Group 17 (Halogens)
  • Chemical stability: Forms compounds easily by gaining one electron
  • Combustibility: Does not burn, but can support reactions with some substances
  • Reaction with water: Reacts with water to form hydrochloric acid and hypochlorous acid

Discovery

Carl Wilhelm Scheele

Chlorine was discovered in 1774 by Swedish chemist Carl Wilhelm Scheele. He produced chlorine gas by reacting manganese dioxide with hydrochloric acid. Scheele initially believed that the gas was a compound containing oxygen. In 1810, English chemist Humphry Davy proved that chlorine was a chemical element and gave it the name “chlorine,” from the Greek word “chloros,” meaning “pale green.”


Usages

Water Purification

Chlorine is widely used to disinfect drinking water and swimming pools by killing harmful bacteria and other microorganisms.

Making PVC

Chlorine is used to produce PVC (polyvinyl chloride), a strong plastic used in pipes, cables, flooring and many other products.

Bleaching

Chlorine compounds are used as bleaching agents to whiten paper, textiles and other materials.
