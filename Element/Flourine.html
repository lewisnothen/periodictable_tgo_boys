Flourine

Flourine

Fluorine is a chemical element with the symbol F and atomic number 9. It is a non-metal and belongs to the halogen group on the periodic table. Fluorine is a pale yellow-green gas at room temperature. It is the most reactive and most electronegative element.

Discovery

Henri Moissan

Fluorine was discovered in 1886 by French chemist Henri Moissan, after many unsuccessful attempts by earlier scientists to isolate it. He successfully produced pure fluorine by electrolysing a mixture of potassium fluoride and hydrogen fluoride. It is named after the Latin word “fluere,” meaning “to flow,” because fluorite, a fluorine-containing mineral, was used as a flux to help metals melt more easily.


Usages

Toothpaste and Dental Care

Fluoride compounds are added to toothpaste because they help strengthen tooth enamel and prevent tooth decay.

Non-Stick Cookware

Fluorine is used to make PTFE (Teflon), a heat-resistant material used as a non-stick coating on cookware.

Nuclear Industry

Fluorine is used to produce uranium hexafluoride (UF₆), which is important for separating uranium isotopes in the nuclear industry.
