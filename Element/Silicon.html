Silicon

Silicon

Silicon is a chemical element with the symbol Si and atomic number 14. It is a metalloid and belongs to Group 14 of the periodic table. Silicon is a hard, brittle solid with a blue-grey appearance. It is one of the most important elements used in modern technology.

Properties

📖

Physical Properties

See the color, test the mass, solid, liquid, or a gas!

Data
  • State: Solid at room temperature
  • Colour: Blue-grey
  • Density: About 2.33 g/cm³
  • Melting point: About 1414°C
  • Boiling point: About 3265°C
🧪

Chemical Properties

See how silicon reacts and forms useful compounds!

Data
  • Reactivity: Relatively low at room temperature
  • Group: Group 14 (Carbon group)
  • Chemical stability: Forms a stable oxide layer when exposed to oxygen
  • Bonding: Forms mainly covalent compounds with other elements
  • Reaction with oxygen: Reacts with oxygen when heated to form silicon dioxide (SiO₂)

Discovery

Jöns Jacob Berzelius

Silicon was discovered in 1824 by Swedish chemist Jöns Jacob Berzelius. He isolated silicon by heating potassium with potassium fluorosilicate and then washing the product with water. The name silicon comes from the Latin word “silex,” meaning “flint,” because silicon is found in many minerals and rocks.


Usages

Computer Chips

Silicon is widely used to make computer chips because its semiconductor properties allow it to control electrical currents in electronic devices.

Solar Panels

Silicon is used in solar cells to convert sunlight into electrical energy, making it an important material for solar panels.

Glass and Construction

Silicon dioxide and other silicon compounds are used to make glass, concrete, ceramics and many construction materials.

Second Most Abundant Element

🦷 Silicon is the second most abundant element in Earth's crust, after oxygen.

🌊 The famous technology region “Silicon Valley” is named after silicon because of its connection to the semiconductor industry.
