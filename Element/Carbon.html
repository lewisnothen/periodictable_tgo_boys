Carbon

Carbon

Carbon is a non-metallic chemical element with the symbol C and atomic number 6. It is one of the most important elements for life because it forms the basic framework of organic compounds. Carbon can form strong bonds with other carbon atoms and many other elements, allowing it to create millions of different compounds. It exists in several forms, including diamond, graphite, and graphene, which have very different properties.

Properties

📖

Physical Properties

See the color, test the mass, solid, liquid, or a gas!

Data
  • State: Solid at room temperature
  • Hardness: Diamond is extremely hard, while graphite is soft and slippery.
  • Boiling Point: About 4,800°C under suitable conditions.
  • Solubility: Generally insoluble in water.
  • Thermal Conductivity: Diamond has very high thermal conductivity.
🧪

Chemical Properties

See the color, test the mass, solid, liquid, or a gas!

Data
  • Reaction with Metals: Carbon can react with some metals at high temperatures to form carbides.
  • Reactivity: Carbon is relatively unreactive at room temperature but becomes more reactive at high temperatures.
  • Valency: Carbon has a valency of 4, so it can form four covalent bonds.
  • Combustion: Burns in oxygen to form CO₂ or CO.
  • Reactivity: Generally less reactive at room temperature.

Discovery

Antoine Lavoisier

Carbon has been known since ancient times because materials such as charcoal, soot, and coal were commonly used. However, Antoine Lavoisier, a French scientist, is generally credited with recognizing carbon as an element in 1789.


Usages

Pencil

✏️ Graphite, a form of carbon, is used in pencil “lead.”

Carbon Fiber

🚗 Carbon fibers are used in bicycles, sports equipment, aircraft, and cars because they are strong and lightweight.

Living Organisms

🧬 Carbon is essential for carbohydrates, proteins, fats, DNA, and other molecules needed for life.

Interesting Facts

Structure

💎 Diamond and graphite are both carbon, but their properties are completely different.

Carbon Cycle

🌍 Carbon continuously moves through the air, water, soil, plants, and animals.

Name Origin

🏷️ “Carbon” comes from the Latin word carbo, meaning coal or charcoal.

Found in Rocks

🪨 Carbon is present in minerals such as limestone and dolomite.

Plants Use Carbon

🌳 Plants absorb carbon dioxide (CO₂) during photosynthesis to make food.
