Phosphorus

Phosphorus

Phosphorus is a chemical element with the symbol P and atomic number 15. It is a non-metal and belongs to Group 15 of the periodic table. Phosphorus exists in several forms, including white, red, and black phosphorus. It is an important element for living organisms and is found in DNA, bones, and teeth.

Properties

📖

Physical Properties

See the color, test the mass, solid, liquid, or a gas!

Data
  • State: Solid at room temperature
  • Colour: White phosphorus is pale yellow; red phosphorus is dark red
  • Appearance: Exists in several allotropes
  • Melting point: About 44.1°C for white phosphorus
  • Boiling point: About 280.5°C for white phosphorus
🧪

Chemical Properties

See the color, test the mass, solid, liquid, or a gas!

Data
  • Reactivity: Reactive non-metal, especially in white phosphorus
  • Group: Group 15 (Nitrogen group)
  • Chemical stability: Red and black phosphorus are more stable than white phosphorus
  • Combustibility: White phosphorus can ignite spontaneously in air
  • Reaction with oxygen: Burns in oxygen to form phosphorus oxides

Discovery

Hennig Brand

Phosphorus was discovered in 1669 by German alchemist Hennig Brand. He discovered it while experimenting with urine in an attempt to find the philosopher's stone. Brand heated and processed the urine, producing a glowing substance that he called phosphorus. The name comes from the Greek words meaning “light-bringer” because white phosphorus glows in the dark.


Usages

Fertilizers

Phosphorus compounds are used in fertilizers because phosphorus is an essential nutrient that helps plants develop strong roots and grow properly.

Safety Matches

Red phosphorus is used on the striking surface of safety matchboxes because it helps the match ignite safely when rubbed against the surface.

Detergents

Phosphorus compounds have been used in some detergents to help soften water and improve cleaning performance.

Interesting Facts

Glows in the Dark

⭐️ White phosphorus can glow in the dark because it slowly reacts with oxygen.
