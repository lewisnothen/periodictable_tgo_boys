Neon

Neon

Neon (Ne) is a noble gas with an atomic number of 10 and an atomic mass of about 20.18 u. It belongs to Group 18 and Period 2 of the periodic table. At room temperature, neon is a colourless, odourless gas. It is very unreactive and is best known for producing a bright reddish-orange glow when an electric current passes through it.

  • Does not easily form compounds with other elements.

  • It is chemically stable under normal conditions.

Discovery

Sir William Ramsay and Morris Travers.

Neon was discovered in 1898 by William Ramsay and Morris Travers while investigating liquefied air. They identified it as a new gas because of its distinctive reddish-orange glow in an electrical discharge. They named it neon, meaning “new.”


Usages

Neon signs

💡 Famous for bright reddish-orange advertising and decorative signs.

Laser

🏭 Neon is used in helium-neon lasers.

Specialized lightingt

⚡️ Used in certain lamps and lighting systems.

Interesting Facts

Noble Gas

💨 Neon is one of the noble gases.

Light

The bright lights commonly called “neon lights” can sometimes use other gases to produce different colours.

Reactivity

🧪 Neon is much less reactive than oxygen or nitrogen.

Liquid Neon

🥶 Liquid neon is extremely cold, with a boiling point around −246°C.

Word History

💡 The word “neon” comes from the Greek word neos, meaning “new.”
